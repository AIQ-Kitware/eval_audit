\section{A Taxonomy of Benchmark Reproduction}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{What produces a score}

A measured benchmark result is produced by four things acting together.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item[Recorded recipe --- what the evaluation intends to measure.] It fixes the question put to the model and how the answer is scored, without reference to any particular model: the scenario, dataset, prompt construction, in-context examples, decoding parameters, and scorer. This is the coordinate a benchmark exists to standardize, and the only one HELM records in full.
  \item[Deployment --- what fixes the function to be computed.] Given the recipe's input, it determines the mathematical object that maps that input to a distribution over outputs: the weights, the model and tokenizer revisions, the chat template, the load precision, any quantization, and the client or engine family the deployment names. Changing it changes what is \emph{meant} to be computed, in exact arithmetic as much as in floating point.
  \item[Execution instrument --- what fixes how that function is numerically realized.] It carries out the computation the deployment specifies without changing what that computation is: the harness version, the serving engine's build and kernels, package versions, hardware, device topology, and batching or caching behavior. In exact arithmetic, changing the instrument would be a no-op.
  \item[Residual --- what nothing fixes.] What remains once the other three are held: sampling randomness, and the nondeterminism an instrument admits without determining --- server-side batch composition varying with other users' traffic being the paradigm case. Unlike the other three, no record could pin it down.
\end{description}

The public HELM record exposes the recipe in full, and within the deployment it exposes names rather than the values behind them: a model is named but not the revision that name resolved to, a deployment is named but not the precision it loaded at. Of the instrument and the residual it exposes almost nothing. Reproduction is therefore an inverse problem. One chooses a deployment and an instrument, replays the recorded recipe, and asks whether the result matches the published one --- and when it does not, which coordinate is responsible. We say a run is identifiable at a stated tolerance when every materially compatible choice of coordinates produces equivalent results under a declared output-, metric-, or conclusion-level criterion.

The four coordinates describe what produced a score; the stored record is the only evidence of it, and the two must not be conflated, because a record has a history the run does not. After the process that wrote it has exited, an artifact can be copied forward unchanged into a later release, rewritten by a migration, or damaged in transit. Evidence can therefore be altered, duplicated, or lost independently of what it records --- which is a question about what a comparison is entitled to treat as an observation, and belongs to the gate below rather than to the decomposition above.

The decomposition dictates how reproduction attempts can be classified, and that classification organizes the rest of the paper. Whether a run can be executed at all is a precondition rather than an outcome, and forms an upstream gate (\S\ref{sec:gate}). Past that gate, an attempt is characterized along two orthogonal axes: which coordinates it puts under test (\S\ref{sec:targets}), and, when its result disagrees with the record, which coordinate accounts for the difference (\S\ref{sec:taxonomy}). Both axes follow from the decomposition rather than being proposed alongside it --- the first partitions the coordinates into those an attempt exercises and those it holds fixed, and the second assigns a disagreement to one of the three controllable coordinates, to the residual, or to no coordinate at all. Neither axis refines the other, and collapsing them is what produces the familiar but uninformative verdict that a benchmark ``did not reproduce.''

\subsection{The eligibility gate}
\label{sec:gate}

Before any disagreement is measured, a run must be executable. Gated models or datasets, revoked download access, missing credentials, unsupported packaging, unavailable judges, and infeasible resource requirements are eligibility and environment filters. They answer ``can we run this?'', not ``did it reproduce?'', and must never be folded into a negative reproduction result. Making this gate explicit --- every excluded run carries a typed exclusion reason --- is what allows a defensible denominator: how many runs were runnable, how many were excluded, and for which reason each.

The gate also screens the evidence, not only the execution. A record can fail to be an admissible observation of the run it names, and for reasons that are invisible until checked. An output carried forward unchanged into a later release is one execution recorded twice, so a comparison that counts both inflates its own denominator: where a corpus republishes a run under a new suite version without re-executing it, the two artifacts are byte-identical, and reading their agreement as two confirmations is an arithmetic error rather than a finding. A specification rewritten by a schema migration no longer states what the producing harness wrote, which can defeat replay under any pinned instrument. A file truncated in transit is not evidence of the run it claims to be. None of these is a disagreement between executions; each is a reason a comparison is not comparing what it appears to, and one corpus in this study exhibits the first two together (\S\ref{sec:classic}). We therefore type them as gate reasons alongside the environment filters, and require independence of records, not merely presence of records, before a pair enters a denominator.

The gate is relative to a target, not to a run. The sharpest case is a model-graded scenario whose judge is no longer served: the judging step is unrepeatable, so the run is ineligible for procedural reproduction, but the judge's per-instance verdicts are themselves part of the released record, and the published aggregate can still be recomputed from them and checked. Such a run is out of scope for the second target of \S\ref{sec:targets} and fully in scope for the first. Treating eligibility as a property of the run alone would discard precisely the evidence that survived, and would understate the auditable fraction of the corpus; we therefore scope every exclusion reason to the target it blocks.

\subsection{Axis 1: what is under test}
\label{sec:targets}

A reproduction attempt must first declare what it is attempting to reproduce. We adapt the methods, results, and inferential trichotomy of \citet{goodman2016does} to a benchmark corpus that publishes per-instance outputs, as described in \S\ref{sec:related}. The three resulting choices each exercise a different subset of the coordinates, and they are not interchangeable:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Artifact reconstruction}: recompute the published aggregates from the released prompts, completions, and metric records, without rerunning a model. Tests the scoring code and the record it is applied to, and nothing on the serving side; since the scoring code is part of the instrument, this target is instrument-free only once that code is pinned by era (\S\ref{sec:eras}). For a model-graded scenario the judge's forward pass is the single step it cannot re-execute: the stored annotation is read as data, and everything downstream of it is recomputed as for any deterministic metric.
  \item \textbf{Procedural reproduction}: execute the recorded recipe under a pinned or explicitly equivalent instrument, and compare instance-level outputs and metrics. Tests the deployment and the instrument.
  \item \textbf{Claim-level robustness}: determine whether the substantive conclusion --- a ranking, or a model-generation improvement --- survives plausible execution substrates even when byte-identical outputs do not.
\end{enumerate}

Targets (2) and (3) correspond to Goodman's methods and inferential reproducibility, the former with its ``same data and tools'' premise necessarily relaxed, since the original tools are what did not survive; target (1) has no counterpart there, and his results reproducibility has none here, because a fixed historical corpus admits no new study.

Released official artifacts remain the historical ground truth for what HELM reported. A rerun assesses the stability and identifiability of the procedure that produced them and does not overwrite the record, which is what licenses us to report a differing rerun as evidence about the instrument rather than as a correction of the official number.

\subsection{Axis 2: why two executions disagree}
\label{sec:taxonomy}

Once the target is fixed and the attempt executes, and once the gate has established that the two records are admissible and independent, an observed disagreement admits one of five causes --- one for each of the three controllable coordinates, plus the residual and an underdetermined case:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Recipe mismatch} --- the recorded recipe differs: prompt construction, in-context examples, decoding, or scorer.
  \item \textbf{Deployment mismatch} --- the deployment differs: weights, model or tokenizer revision, chat template, load precision, quantization, or the client, endpoint, and engine family, as when a hosted API is replaced by a local one, or in-process \texttt{transformers} by vLLM.
  \item \textbf{Execution-instrument mismatch} --- the instrument differs: harness or package versions, the engine build, attention kernel, device placement, hardware, or batching and caching behavior. This includes the shape of the record the harness writes, measured corpus-wide in \S\ref{sec:coverage}.
  \item \textbf{Residual disagreement under controlled equivalence} --- every controllable coordinate is matched and a gap remains. This is the only category analogous to conventional repeatability failure.
  \item \textbf{Historically non-identifiable reproduction} --- the surviving evidence does not uniquely specify what execution should count as faithful. Qualitatively different from (4): not ``it failed to repeat'' but ``what would count as a faithful execution is underdetermined.''
\end{enumerate}

The two axes are independent: any target can fail for any cause, and it is their combination, not either alone, that describes an outcome. We deliberately avoid the phrase ``true reproducibility failure'' whenever execution provenance is incomplete. Categories (1) through (3) are usually recoverable by controlling the substrate, and (5) is a first-class result rather than a gap to be closed.

\subsection{Per-parameter identifiability}
\label{sec:identifiability}

Identifiability in the sense fixed above --- equivalent results, within a stated tolerance, across every materially compatible configuration --- is not a binary property of an entire run. Each latent parameter within the deployment and the instrument has its own status, and the per-case analysis assigns one to each (Table~\ref{tab:identifiability}). This refinement is what makes both the positive attributions and the non-identifiability result precise: we never claim to identify a whole historical deployment, only specific parameters within it, each with its own evidentiary basis.

A coordinate can fail to be recoverable in two ways, and the difference decides what can be done about it. It may be structurally unrecoverable, in that no record of this form could pin it down, or merely practically unrecoverable, in that the particular record which happens to survive is insufficient \citep{raue2009identifiability}. Our classic-model result (\S\ref{sec:classic}) is the second kind, and that is what makes the provenance recommendation of \S\ref{sec:provenance} actionable: a practically non-identifiable parameter is one a richer record would have recovered, whereas a structurally non-identifiable one would leave a recording standard nothing to fix. We therefore state that result as non-identifiability relative to named evidence rather than as a claim of irreproducibility.
