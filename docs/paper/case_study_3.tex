We show how \eee{} results can be used to verify instance-level reproducibility. Despite no promises from framework providers, many run their own experiments locally and compare them to the globally shared results; thus, analyzing reproducibility is of value.
We performed a local reproduction of 
Pythia-6.9B \citep{pmlr-v202-biderman23a}, Vicuna-7B v1.3 \citep{vicuna2023}, and Falcon-7B \citep{almazrouei2023falcon} results on fourteen
  single-turn HELM benchmarks\citep{clark-etal-2019-boolq,
borkan2019nuanced,
mei2021capturing,
konda2016magellan,
kopcke2010evaluation,
cobbe2021training,
maas-etal-2011-learning,
zhong2021arlsat,
mmlu,
kocisky-etal-2018-narrativeqa,
choi-etal-2018-quac,
wu2021lime,
clark2020transformers,
lin-etal-2022-truthfulqa,
petroni-etal-2019-language%
} and compared them to the \emph{official} HELM-released records
  ~\citep{helm}, for each we report the percentage of same-score examples (more details in App.~\ref{app:case3}).
  We reported our results in the \eee{} format and compared them, finding mismatched scores, as well as mismatched examples.

Figure~\ref{fig:cs3-helm-heatmap} summarizes the comparison results and reveals four non-reproducibility patterns:
  non-comparability, serving artifacts, stochastic disagreement, and residual
  answer differences.

\paragraph{Incomparable examples in Entity-Matching.}
HELM evaluated models on 1{,}000 Abt--Buy~\cite{kopcke2010evaluation} examples, but
  despite using the same recipe, the 1{,}000 examples in the reproduction did not match the original ones.
Evidently, pandas versions have changed since HELM was released, and the pandas merge row-order update made the HELM framework choose different examples~\citep{mckinney2010data,pandas_merge_docs}. In conclusion, the instance-level inputs allowed us to find a large source of inconsistency that otherwise would have been hidden. 

\paragraph{Serving artifacts in SyntheticReasoning-Natural.}
For Pythia, official outputs are empty and as may be expected, the scores are zero, whereas
  our reproduction records completions with low but non-zero scores.
Analyzing the difference revealed that the original outputs begin with a newline stop symbol and the rest was thus erroneously trimmed before scoring.
This likely reflects deployment differences: HELM v0.3.0 used Together.ai,
  whereas ours used local Hugging Face inference.
\eee{} does not encode the historical serving stack, but surfaces the empty scored completions and the difference in deployment engines.

\paragraph{Disagreement due to stochasticity in WikiFact.}
We find approximately 92\% of the examples in WikiFact \citep{petroni-etal-2019-language} have the same score, this is an expected failure in the reproduction as the model running is not deterministic, and uses sampling (\texttt{temperature=1.0} and \texttt{num\_outputs=5}).
This slightly exceeds what one might expect from just sampling (see the homogeneous-Bernoulli prediction in Appendix~\ref{sec:app-helm-wikifact}), but is consistent with prompt-dependent difficulty.

\paragraph{Residual disagreements are real but smaller.}
Beyond those large differences, which we can explain, smaller unexplained ones remain. Pythia-6.9B is, in general, less consistent, which may be more sensitive to serving-stack
  changes, but the audit cannot separate model strength from deployment details.
Thus, \eee{} detects failure modes and provides forensics, but does not replace
  framework-level provenance.
